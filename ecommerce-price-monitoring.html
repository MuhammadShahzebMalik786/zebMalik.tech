The problem

Manual price checking fails in a predictable way.

It's always out of date

Someone checks the top 30 SKUs once a month. By the time the sheet is filled in, half the prices have moved and nobody trusts it enough to act on.

It doesn't scale past a few SKUs

Checking 3,000 products by hand isn't a task anyone completes. So teams check the products they already worry about — and miss the ones quietly losing.

It hides the interesting part

A price today tells you little. A price history tells you when they discount, how deep, and how long they hold it. Manual checking never produces that.

What we deliver

A dated row for every product, every run.

Because each run is timestamped and stored, you get a price history as a by-product — so you can see discount patterns, not just today's number.

Questions

Before you ask.

Is scraping competitor prices legal?

We collect publicly accessible pricing — the same pages any shopper can see — and respect each site's terms and rate limits. We do not access anything behind a login we aren't authorised for. Competitive price research is common practice, but how you use the data is your decision, and if it involves personal data rather than product data, check your obligations under GDPR or your local equivalent.

What happens when they change their site?

Selectors break — that's normal, and fixing them is included in every monthly plan. You'll usually know because we've already fixed it, not because your feed went empty.

Can it write into our existing system?

Yes. Google Sheets, S3, PostgreSQL, MySQL, Airtable, or a webhook into whatever you already run.

How fast can it be running?

A single competitor snapshot is usually same-day. A full scheduled feed across several competitors is typically live within a week.
